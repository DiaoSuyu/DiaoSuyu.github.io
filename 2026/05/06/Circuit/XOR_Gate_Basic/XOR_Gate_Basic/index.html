[Digital Circuit] XOR Gate Implemented with Basic Logic Gates

Posted on 2026-05-06 In Digital Circuit , Circuit Design Views:
3.4k 3 mins.

This topic records the design, implementation and verification of a standard XOR gate circuit in Logisim, including circuit structure, logical expression, truth table and step-by-step operation.

1. Overview

This note records how to build and verify a classic XOR (Exclusive OR) gate using basic logic gates (NOT, AND, OR) in Logisim. It covers:

  • Circuit construction in Logisim
  • Deriving the logical expression from the circuit diagram
  • Verifying the truth table

2. Symbol Reference

Symbol Meaning Example
$x$, $y$ Boolean input variables (0 or 1) $x = 1$
$\neg x$ NOT $x$ — logical negation (inverts the value) $\neg 1 = 0$
$x \cdot y$ $x$ AND $y$ — true only when both are 1 $1 \cdot 0 = 0$
$x + y$ $x$ OR $y$ — true when at least one is 1 $0 + 1 = 1$
$x \oplus y$ $x$ XOR $y$ — true only when inputs differ $1 \oplus 1 = 0$
$x \odot y$ $x$ XNOR $y$ — true only when inputs are the same $1 \odot 1 = 1$
$\overline{x \odot y}$ NOT of XNOR — equivalent to XOR $\overline{x \odot y} = x \oplus y$

3. Circuit Structure in Logisim

The circuit consists of the following components built in Logisim:

  • 2 inputs: x and y (Logisim “Input Pin”)
  • 2 NOT gates (Not1, Not2)
  • 2 AND gates (And1, And2)
  • 1 OR gate (Or1)
  • 1 output: o (Logisim “Output Pin”)

The signal flow is as follows:

  1. x and y are fed into the AND gates both directly and through NOT gates.
  2. The outputs of the two AND gates are combined by the OR gate to produce the final result.

4. Logical Expression Derivation

We derive the expression step by step from the diagram:

Step 1: Output of And1

And1 takes x and inverted y ($\neg y$) as inputs.

$$
And_1 = x \cdot \neg y
$$

Step 2: Output of And2

And2 takes inverted x ($\neg x$) and y as inputs.

$$
And_2 = \neg x \cdot y
$$

Step 3: Final Output o

Or1 combines the outputs of And1 and And2.

$$
o = Or1 = And_1 + And_2 = (x \cdot \neg y) + (\neg x \cdot y)
$$

This is the canonical form of the XOR operation, written as:

$$
o = x \oplus y
$$

5. Truth Table Verification in Logisim

Verify the circuit by toggling inputs and observing outputs:

$x$ $y$ $\neg x$ $\neg y$ $A_1 = x \cdot \neg y$ $A_2 = \neg x \cdot y$ $o = x \oplus y$
0 0 1 1 0 0 0
0 1 1 0 0 1 1
1 0 0 1 1 0 1
1 1 0 0 0 0 0

The output o is 1 when x and y differ, and 0 when they are the same. This confirms a valid XOR gate.

Appendix: Simplified Boolean Algebra

The XOR expression can also be written in a simpler form:

$$
x \oplus y = \overline{x \odot y} = (x + y) \cdot (\neg x + \neg y)
$$

This form uses one OR, one AND, and two NOT gates, and is functionally equivalent.

Derivation

We verify that $(x + y) \cdot (\neg x + \neg y) = x \oplus y$ by expanding the left side step by step.

Step 1: Expand using the distributive law $(a + b) \cdot (c + d) = a \cdot c + a \cdot d + b \cdot c + b \cdot d$:

$$
(x + y) \cdot (\neg x + \neg y) = x \cdot \neg x + x \cdot \neg y + y \cdot \neg x + y \cdot \neg y
$$

Step 2: Apply the complement law $a \cdot \neg a = 0$ to eliminate the first and last terms:

$$
= 0 + x \cdot \neg y + \neg x \cdot y + 0
$$

Step 3: Simplify:

$$
= (x \cdot \neg y) + (\neg x \cdot y)
$$

Step 4: This is exactly the canonical XOR form derived in Section 4:

$$
= x \oplus y \quad \checkmark
$$

Relationship to XNOR

The XNOR operation ($x \odot y$) is the logical complement of XOR. We derive its canonical form starting from the simplified XOR expression proved above:

$$
x \oplus y = (x + y) \cdot (\neg x + \neg y)
$$

Take NOT on both sides:

$$
\neg(x \oplus y) = \neg[(x + y) \cdot (\neg x + \neg y)]
$$

Step 1: Apply De Morgan’s law $\neg(A \cdot B) = \neg A + \neg B$:

$$
= \neg(x + y) + \neg(\neg x + \neg y)
$$

Step 2: Apply De Morgan’s law $\neg(A + B) = \neg A \cdot \neg B$ to each term:

$$
= (\neg x \cdot \neg y) + (x \cdot y)
$$

Step 3: Reorder:

$$
\neg(x \oplus y) = (x \cdot y) + (\neg x \cdot \neg y)
$$

This is the canonical form of XNOR — it outputs 1 only when both inputs are the same. Therefore:

$$
x \odot y = \neg(x \oplus y) = (x \cdot y) + (\neg x \cdot \neg y)
$$

And conversely, XOR can be written as:

$$
x \oplus y = \overline{x \odot y}
$$

This means an XOR gate can equivalently be built from a single XNOR gate followed by a NOT gate.
